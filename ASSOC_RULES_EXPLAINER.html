Association Rule Analysis

Also called affinity analysis or market basket analysis: a "what goes with what" technique that mines If-Then rules — like {diapers, beer} ⇒ {chips} — from transaction data. This explainer covers the Apriori algorithm's pruning logic, and how to compute and interpret support, confidence, and the lift ratio.

Our working dataset — 16 grocery transactions across 6 items
💡 Association rule analysis is unsupervised — there's no target variable. It just counts how often items co-occur across transactions and turns frequent co-occurrences into If-Then rules.
🛒 Beyond retail: streaming services use the same logic to recommend shows based on co-viewing patterns, and in health care, association rules can surface symptoms that tend to occur together.
Combinatorial explosion — possible itemsets grow fast with n items
6
⚠️ Number of possible If-Then combinations = 3n − 2(n+1) + 1. Even a modest 100-item convenience store implies about 5.15 × 10⁴⁷ combinations — far too many to brute-force.
Apriori pruning in action — drag the minimum support threshold
0.25
✅ Apriori exploits one rule: if an itemset is infrequent, every larger itemset that contains it must also be infrequent. So it only builds bigger candidate itemsets out of the smaller itemsets that already survived the support cutoff — raising the threshold prunes the search tree dramatically.
